# PACI BOSS™ — Design Specification
> Small mouth. BIG business.
>
> Official motto: GOO GOO GAGA.
## 1. Project Overview
PACI BOSS™ is a fictional pacifier company run by an overly ambitious baby CEO.
The website should look as if a toddler tried to build a billion-dollar company using a school notebook, crayons, cardboard, sticky tape, highlighters, and unreasonable confidence.
The entire website must feel handmade, playful, imperfect, and illustrated by a child.
This is NOT a conventional corporate website with childish decorations added to it. The visual identity itself must communicate the concept.
### Character Identity
- **Brand:** PACI BOSS™
- **Tagline:** Small mouth. BIG business.
- **Official motto:** GOO GOO GAGA.
- **Character:** A cute, confident, dramatic baby CEO.
- **Business:** Selling pacifiers.
- **Personality:** Adorable, greedy, ambitious, overconfident, and easily distracted by milk and naps.
- **Weaknesses:** Milk, naps, tantrums, and being unable to reach the top shelf.
- **Signature appearance:** Oversized black sunglasses, a tiny golden crown, and a blue pacifier.
### Motto Translations
The official motto remains GOO GOO GAGA.
Its humorous translations can appear throughout the website:
- Small mouth. BIG business.
- Nap hard. Dream bigger.
- Less talk. More pacifiers.
- Born to crawl. Built to scale.
- Milk is temporary. The empire is forever.
Add occasional handwritten annotations suggesting that these translations were approved by the CEO himself.

## 2. Overall Art Direction
### Core Visual Concept
A children's doodle notebook meets a handmade cardboard business office.
The website should feel like a real notebook filled with colourful drawings, scribbles, school supplies, cardboard documents, and ridiculous business plans.
The design should be expressive but organised. Every decorative element must support the playful identity without making the content difficult to read.
### Background
Use a warm, off-white paper background with:
- Extremely subtle paper grain.
- Faint pencil marks.
- Occasional crayon scribbles.
- Small hand-drawn doodles.
- Imperfect, handmade details.
Avoid perfectly flat digital backgrounds.
The paper texture must remain subtle enough to preserve readability.
### Colour Palette
| Colour | Hex | Primary Use |
|---|---|---|
| Paper cream | `#FFF8E8` | Main background |
| Crayon pink | `#FF719A` | Highlights, accents, playful elements |
| Marker blue | `#55B9F5` | Product illustrations and accents |
| Sunshine yellow | `#FFD84D` | Highlighter strokes and emphasis |
| Lime green | `#B8E986` | Small accents and decorative elements |
| Crayon red | `#FF5B55` | Stamps, warnings, and important details |
| Ink black | `#292521` | Main text and outlines |
| Cardboard brown | `#C89B68` | Handmade documents and statistics |
Cream should dominate the page.
Use bright colours selectively for illustrations, highlighted words, doodles, buttons, product cards, and handmade paper elements.
Maintain sufficient contrast between text and background.
Do not use every colour equally.

## 3. Drawing and Doodle Language
Hand-drawn details must be a fundamental part of the composition.
### Required Doodles
- Uneven five-pointed stars.
- Little hearts and smiley faces.
- Hand-drawn arrows.
- Wavy underlines.
- Imperfect circles.
- Crayon scribbles.
- Tiny crowns.
- Milk bottles.
- Pacifiers.
- Baby-related doodles.
- Handwritten side comments.
- Roughly outlined speech bubbles.
- Crossed-out words.
- Childlike graphs.
- Nonsensical business charts.
- Tiny pencil sketches.
### Drawing Style
Use imperfect strokes with slightly irregular line thickness.
Drawings should feel spontaneous rather than geometrically perfect.
Place doodles around content, between sections, and near important labels.
Use slight rotations and imperfect alignment to create a handmade appearance.
Do not fill every available space.
Doodles must never overlap essential text, buttons, or navigation.
### Highlighter Effects
Use rough marker strokes behind important words and phrases.
Highlighter strokes should:
- Extend slightly beyond the text.
- Have irregular edges.
- Look hand-drawn rather than perfectly geometric.
- Use pink, yellow, blue, or lime.
- Appear selectively throughout the page.
Avoid highlighting every heading.
### Cardboard Elements
Company documents must look handmade.
Use:
- Rough-cut edges.
- Visible corrugated cardboard textures where appropriate.
- Imperfect marker lettering.
- Crayon signatures.
- Uneven outlines.
- Illustrated tape.
- Crooked stamps.
- Slight rotations.
Do not style cardboard documents as polished digital cards with simple brown backgrounds.

## 4. Typography
Use three complementary typographic styles.
### Display Typography
Use a playful handwritten font for major headings.
Headings should feel expressive, irregular, and childlike.
### Body Typography
Use a rounded, highly readable sans-serif font for paragraphs, navigation, product descriptions, and longer text.
Do not use an irregular handwritten font for all body text.
### Emphasis Typography
Use chunky, toy-block-inspired lettering for selected labels:
- CEO
- TOP SECRET
- MY EMPIRE
- OFFICIAL
- 100% LEGIT
The lettering may reference colourful children's building blocks, but must not use actual LEGO branding or logos.
### Typography Rules
- Use oversized headings selectively.
- Highlight important words with rough marker strokes.
- Use handwritten annotations sparingly.
- Maintain a clear visual hierarchy.
- Ensure text remains readable on mobile.
- Avoid excessive font variety.

## 10. The CEO's Biography
### Section Heading
THE MAN. THE MYTH. THE MILK.
Present the biography as a handwritten autobiography on a large notebook page.
### Biography Copy
They say Rome wasn't built in a day.
Neither was I.
It took me approximately one year to stand on my own two legs. For the first two years of my life, I couldn't even communicate properly. My vocabulary consisted of crying, random noises, and strategically timed screaming.
But I had a vision.
While other babies were busy playing with their toys, I was studying the pacifier market. While others were sleeping, I was also sleeping—but with ambition.
Then came the breakthrough: my first pacifier.
I looked at it and thought, "Goo goo gaga."
The world heard baby noises. I saw a business opportunity.
Today, I am the founder, chairman, CEO, and only employee of PACI BOSS™.
We're building an empire, one pacifier at a time.
I may not know how taxes work. I may still need help opening a juice box. But one thing is certain:
**I was born to lead. I just needed someone to carry me to the office.**
### Visual Treatment
The biography should feel like a child's diary transformed into a ridiculously serious corporate origin story.
Include:
- Notebook-paper texture.
- Small pencil doodles.
- A baby fingerprint.
- A crayon signature.
- Handwritten annotations.
- A hand-drawn arrow pointing toward the final punchline.
Highlight the final sentence using a bold pink highlighter stroke.
Keep the text readable and avoid placing doodles over paragraphs.

## 15. Interactions and Animations
Keep interactions lightweight and realistic for a 48-hour hackathon.
### Product Hover Effects
Product cards can wobble or tilt slightly when hovered.
Keep the movement subtle.
### Button Interactions
Buttons can move slightly or reveal a playful highlighter effect on hover.
Provide clear visual feedback when tapped on mobile.
### Baby Character Interaction
Clicking the baby character can reveal a small speech bubble.
Possible dialogue:
"Goo goo gaga!"
"WHERE IS MY MILK?"
"YOU MAY ADDRESS ME AS SIR."
### Smooth Scrolling
Navigation links should scroll smoothly to their corresponding sections.
Ensure section headings are not hidden behind a sticky header.
### Easter Egg Interaction
Clicking the hidden doodle should reveal the secret CEO file.
Provide a clear way to dismiss the revealed content.
### Animation Constraints
Avoid:
- Heavy 3D effects.
- Complex physics.
- Expensive animations.
- Excessive motion.
- Unnecessary interactive systems.
Animations should enhance the handmade personality rather than distract from the content.
Respect reduced-motion preferences.

## 17. Responsive Design
The website must work well on desktop, tablet, and mobile.
### Desktop
- Use expressive compositions.
- Position doodles around the main content.
- Allow selective overlaps between decorative paper pieces.
- Maintain clear spacing and visual hierarchy.
- Give the main illustrations enough space.
### Tablet
- Reduce excessive overlaps.
- Reposition doodles.
- Adapt product cards to available width.
- Preserve readable text and clear navigation.
### Mobile
- Use a single-column layout where appropriate.
- Stack cardboard statistics and timeline elements vertically.
- Keep the baby character and hero heading prominent.
- Reduce decorative doodles.
- Remove unnecessary overlaps.
- Use touch-friendly navigation.
- Ensure buttons are easy to tap.
- Keep product descriptions and biography text readable.
- Prevent horizontal overflow.
- Never allow decorative elements to cover essential text or controls.
Do not simply shrink the desktop design.
Compose a deliberate mobile version of the same visual identity.
### Responsive Quality Checklist
- No horizontal scrolling.
- No overlapping essential text.
- No clipped buttons.
- No unreadably small text.
- No inaccessible navigation.
- No decorative elements blocking interactions.
- No excessively large images causing layout problems.
---
## 18. Design Consistency
Maintain the same visual language throughout the entire page.
Every section should look as though it belongs to the same handmade notebook.
Use consistent:
- Paper textures.
- Crayon and marker outlines.
- Highlighter colours.
- Handwritten annotations.
- Cardboard materials.
- Baby character illustrations.
- Rounded, playful typography.
Individual elements can vary naturally without making the overall design inconsistent.
### Avoid
- Generic SaaS dashboards.
- Conventional corporate gradients.
- Glassmorphism.
- Glossy 3D cards.
- Excessive drop shadows.
- Perfectly symmetrical card layouts everywhere.
- Stock photographs.
- Photorealistic babies.
- Random decorative elements unrelated to the brand.
- Overcrowded layouts.
- Illegible handwritten body text.
- Unnecessary sections.
- Complicated features that cannot reasonably be completed in 48 hours.

## 19. Hackathon Requirements
The design must support the following requirements from the Baby's First Website challenge brief.
### Character Introduction
Clearly establish who PACI BOSS is, his personality, and what he does.
### Three Showcase Projects
Include at least three explicit showcase entries:
1. Paci Empire — the fictional pacifier product collection.
2. Baby Boss Business Plan — the company's fictional business plan, statistics, and identity.
3. Goo Goo Gaga Brand — the fictional brand identity, product branding, or advertising campaign.
These are portfolio entries within the fictional character's website. They do not need to be three separate applications.
### Origin Story
Include the CEO biography and timeline.
Ensure the origin story is presented as an identifiable narrative rather than only scattered jokes.
If the submitted implementation separates the story into scenes, keep each scene under 150 words, as required by the supplied brief.
### Interactive Moment
At least one working interaction is required.
The baby speech bubble, product hover effect, or hidden CEO file can provide this interaction.
### Responsive Layout
The website must function on mobile and desktop.
### Hidden Easter Egg
Include the secret CEO file.
### Functional Quality
Prioritise working navigation, readable content, reliable interactions, and responsive behaviour over complicated effects.

## 22. Final Creative Principle
Every element should feel as though a baby tried to build a Fortune 500 company using a cardboard box, crayons, and absolutely unreasonable confidence.
The website should not merely look cute.
The humour, company documents, product showcase, typography, character, and interactions must all reinforce the same story.
The contrast between childish handmade visuals and serious corporate language is the core creative idea.
### Final Design Goal
Create a distinctive, memorable, fully responsive website that feels like a child's hand-drawn notebook documenting a ridiculously ambitious pacifier company.
The website should be:
- Playful.
- Colourful.
- Handmade.
- Funny.
- Expressive.
- Visually coherent.
- Readable.
- Responsive.
- Achievable within a 48-hour hackathon.
The final result should make visitors think:
**"A BABY ACTUALLY BUILT A WHOLE COMPANY LIKE THIS?"**
